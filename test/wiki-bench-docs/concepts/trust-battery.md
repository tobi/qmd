# Trust Battery

## Definition

The trust battery is Tobi’s metaphor for the accumulated confidence that lets people work with less supervision. It charges when someone makes good decisions, follows through, gives useful feedback, and behaves reliably under pressure. It drains when commitments are broken, work is hidden, or a person repeatedly requires others to compensate for them. Trust is gradual rather than binary, and a charged battery buys autonomy: people can own a larger area because others expect them to handle it well.

The metaphor turns a vague social quality into something a team can discuss without pretending it is perfectly measurable. A new hire does not begin with unlimited trust, but neither do they begin at zero; the relationship changes through observable work. The same logic applies between Shopify and investors, partners, and merchants. Trust is an asset that expands the organization’s room to act, but it can plateau or be spent faster than it is replenished.

Trust battery is not obedience or popularity. Someone can disagree, challenge a decision, or make a mistake while still charging trust if they are candid and responsible. Conversely, surface agreement can conceal a depleted battery. The useful question is whether the person’s pattern of behavior gives others more confidence to delegate consequential work.

## Source-grounded synthesis

- **Trust creates economy:** In `SRC-2017-08-04-001`, Tobi says a charged interpersonal battery creates “economy”—the ability to move faster, receive leadership opportunities, and operate with less explanation. He extends the same metaphor to Shopify’s relationship with public investors.
- **Trust is a gradient:** `SRC-2018-09-18-001` makes the model explicit: trust starts as a partial prior, rises through collaboration, and eventually supports an area of ownership. It is a process of earned autonomy rather than a one-time promotion.
- **Recognition can make trust visible:** `SRC-2011-09-07-001` describes peer-awarded recognition through Shopify’s Unicorn system. The mechanism is not identical to the battery, but it shows an early attempt to let contribution and social credit circulate through the organization.
- **The metaphor has limits:** `SRC-2022-11-15-001` does not revisit the term despite the page’s earlier expectation. That absence matters: the battery is a useful local language, not a complete theory of trust or a permanent Shopify doctrine.

## Tensions and limits

Trust language can become subjective, opaque, or a way to reward similarity to the people already in power. A manager should be able to explain what behavior builds trust and give people a fair chance to demonstrate it. The source material supports trust as an operating metaphor, but it does not justify treating a private impression as an objective score.

## Timeline

> How Tobi framed this across appearances (date → source → framing note).

- **2011-09-07** — [[sources/2011-business-of-software]] — Unnamed precursor: Tobi describes Shopify's "unicorn" peer-recognition pool (1% of revenue, peer-awarded) as a system that lets social credit accumulate across the company — the interpersonal-trust substrate the "battery" metaphor will later name, but the battery framing is not yet used.
- **2017-08-04** — [[sources/2017-motley-fool-tom-gardner]] — Explicitly named: Tobi introduces the "trust battery" — it charges with good work and, once charged, buys economy and leadership positions; he extends the metaphor beyond people to the Shopify↔investor relationship, arguing that investor battery "fills up" (a reason to go public rather than keep raising privately).
- **2018-09-18** — [[sources/2018-knowledge-project]] — Popularized and mechanized: on KP Ep. 41 (titled "the Trust Battery") Tobi frames trust as a gradient (not on/off), starting ~50% at hire, charging through collaboration and depleting/plateauing through no-shows; charged trust is the explicit currency for autonomy — "get to 80–90% … then they give you an area to own."
- **2022-11-15** — [[sources/2022-knowledge-project-152]] — Not revisited (low confidence): the term "trust battery" is not invoked in the 2022 return to Farnam Street; the conversation moves to "calm progress" and tech-optimism, so the source page's "four-year update" framing is not borne out by the transcript.
