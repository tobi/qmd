# The Knowledge Project - Episode 41 (Tobi Lütke, the Trust Battery)

> Raw packet: [[sources/2018-knowledge-project]]

## Summary

An in-person Ottawa interview with Shane Parrish for Farnam Street's Knowledge Project, when Shopify had ~600,000 merchants and ~4,000 employees. The conversation ranges widely: Tobi's argument that video games (StarCraft, Factorio) are distilled deliberate-practice environments that taught him to run a company; the snowboard origin (he kept and bought back the original Snowdevil boards as office decoration); deliberately headquartering outside Silicon Valley as a [[concepts/secondary-markets|secondary-markets]] strategy; scaling from 2 to 4,000 people; the [[concepts/trust-battery|trust battery]] and the "Tobi test"; quick vs analytic decision-making; and an optimistic take on AI/ML.

## Key Points

- Video games as deliberate practice: StarCraft is a symmetrical, imperfect-information, real-time decision game; the scarce resource is **attention**, not in-game minerals — "I think I actually learned most about building businesses from playing Starcraft." Factorio "will teach you operational excellence by accident."
- The [[concepts/trust-battery|trust battery]]: you join a company and charge a trust battery by doing good work; a charged battery buys autonomy and leadership. Making "trust" a named metaphor lets the org talk about it.
- Secondary-market strategy: Shopify deliberately HQ'd outside Silicon Valley; hires high-future-potential people and trains internally rather than buying industry insiders.
- The "Tobi test" is used to keep teams adaptable, unified, and prepared when things go wrong (referenced in the episode description).
- Decision-making philosophy contrasts quick vs analytic decisions; Tobi leans on mental models and a deliberate morning routine.
- Optimistic on AI/ML as a force multiplier for the work, not a threat.
