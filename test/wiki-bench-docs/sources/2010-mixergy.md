# How Shopify Became Profitable By Helping Anyone Open An Online Boutique (Mixergy)

> Raw packet: [[sources/2010-mixergy]]

## Summary

A 2010 Mixergy interview conducted about four years after launch, when Shopify served ~6,000 merchants (including Pixar, Foo Fighters, Tesla Motors, Amnesty International) and had just hit its first multi-million-dollar year. Tobi traces the Snowdevil snowboard-store origin, the frustration with Yahoo Stores / osCommerce, discovering Ruby on Rails in August 2004, and — most centrally — the pricing-model pivot from a pure take-rate to subscription-plus-low-transaction-fee, which he calls the "true launch" of the product.

## Key Points

- The original business model was backwards: heavy users would have paid far too much under a flat take rate, while casual users got it nearly free — so Shopify was turning away its best customers. Switching to a subscription + much lower transaction fee "was the magic" that unlocked growth.
- Paying money "focuses the mind"; a free-until-ready model actually reduces incentive to make the business succeed.
- Founder naivety is an asset: "a complete disregard of the poor odds" of success; being too experienced can cause problems.
- Audience-first launch: building the open-source Typo weblog engine in Rails (~10,000 installs, each linking back to his blog) gave Tobi a ready audience for the 2006 Shopify launch.
- Origin story fixed points: moved to Canada in 2002, German company he worked for folded, started Snowdevil to combine snowboarding and programming, hated existing ecommerce tools.
- First million-dollar year in 2008; "multi-million-dollar year" by 2010.
