# The Rise of Shopify (Kara Swisher, Startupfest 2016)

> Raw packet: [[sources/2016-kara-swisher]]

## Summary

A post-IPO fireside with Kara Swisher at Startupfest in Montreal (2016). Tobi recounts starting a snowboard store in 2004 during the "nuclear winter" of ecommerce, the ~2010 decision to rebuild Shopify for a multichannel world ("like rebuilding an airplane mid-flight"), and Shopify's positioning as the invisible back office behind other surfaces — including Facebook buy buttons and even Amazon listings. The through-line is that Shopify powers unique, non-commodity commerce ("stuff that doesn't come with a barcode") and that a major tech company can be built in a secondary market like Canada with different (longer-tenure) assumptions than the Silicon Valley playbook.

## Key Points

- 2004 was ecommerce "nuclear winter" (dotcom/pets.com crash); Shopify began as Snowdevil, became a profitable lifestyle company before taking venture capital.
- Around 2010 Shopify rebuilt its tech "guts" for multichannel: one store sign-up maps to many channels (web, Facebook, mobile app, Amazon) rather than exactly one online store — "rebuilding an airplane mid-flight."
- Facebook buy buttons are "almost universally a Shopify store behind that"; the transaction is only the first 10% — the messy back office (inventory, drop-shipping, taxes, workflows) is Shopify's real moat.
- On Amazon: "if you're not afraid of Amazon you don't understand Amazon" — but Shopify is merchant-aligned and invisible by design; merchants often fulfill Amazon orders via Shopify too.
- Commerce thesis: Shopify's winners are unique/curated goods, "not the things that usually have barcodes" (e.g. Pokémon-Go-inspired jewelry trending that week).
- [[concepts/secondary-markets|Secondary markets]]: building in Canada means different assumptions — e.g. a 5-year staff tenure baseline vs Silicon Valley — which changes how you hire and treat staff.
- Shopify stayed deliberately neutral (won't invest in merchants / become kingmaker) — a recurring [[concepts/platform-neutrality|platform neutrality]] theme.

## Counter-arguments

- Swisher pushes the "is there really an alternative to Amazon?" skepticism; Tobi's answer (work with Amazon as a channel) sidesteps whether independent merchants can durably win.
- "Non-barcode" thesis is rhetorically clean but the boundary blurs as Shopify adds larger merchants and fulfillment.
