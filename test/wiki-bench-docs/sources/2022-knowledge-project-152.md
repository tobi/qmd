# Tobi Lütke: Calm Progress (The Knowledge Project Ep. 152)

> Raw packet: [[sources/2022-knowledge-project-152]]

## Summary
A 2022 return to Shane Parrish's Knowledge Project (Ep. 41 in 2018 was the trust-battery / video-games episode). The framing is "calm progress" — keeping the most important thing the most important thing through COVID's volatility, notably by *subtracting* ~60% of everything R&D was working on. Founder vs professional-manager companies are drawn as "spiky volcanic rock" vs "smooth river stone," and Tobi argues business should be re-derived from software-engineering principles — "applied computational philosophy." Note: unlike Ep. 41, the trust battery is **not** named in this transcript.

## Key Points
- "Calm progress": keep the North Star fixed (make entrepreneurship simpler); roadmaps are overrated — rerun the "what's most helpful now" function every time a team is ready, rather than doggedly following a plan. Cancelled ~60% of R&D work in early COVID using founder legitimacy.
- Founders' superpower is *subtraction* — "much easier to add than remove"; sediment layers of individually-good additions build into nonsense. Founder legitimacy is a "bank account" deposited by the founding story that only founders can draw on (cites Vitalik's essay on legitimacy); Nadella at Microsoft as a rare non-founder who subtracts.
- Founder-led vs professionally-managed companies: "spiky volcanic rock" (aptitudes matched to the company's real problems, surprising weaknesses) vs "smooth river stone" (stakeholder management, operational excellence, reversion-to-mean competence). Best practices = "average, risk-adjusted" — no time for average.
- Risk: "the one thing that doesn't take risk is doing what everyone else does"; at Shopify "it's more risky to not take risks" for one's career. Psychological safety to fail is hard to build and maintain.
- Comp: rebuilt Shopify's comp — everyone gets one agreed number, then chooses cash/stock sliders (future: charity bucket). Tying lifetime earnings to a volatile stock price (e.g., a Russia-invasion-Ukraine week before a grant) is "a patent violation."
- "All of business has to be re-derived from programming principles"; computing is "applied computational philosophy" (Wittgenstein as "the first hacker"); engineering's systems-thinking is ~100 years ahead of orthodox business management.
- Decision-making is easy; finding the *inputs* is hard. Resulting (poker term) ≠ good decisions. Secondary/tertiary effects often opposite of primary (e.g., environmental movement's anti-nuclear founding myth → climate change).
- Sleep fix via CBT-I (CBT for insomnia); "sleep is like hunger" — go to bed only when sleepy, bed is only for sleeping; fixed his in 3 days after years of sleeping pills. Critical of *Why We Sleep*'s anxiety-inducing 8-hours-for-all claim.
- AI/ML optimism: stable diffusion's open-sourcing "found 2-3 more gears"; ML progressing faster than his model of it; Turing machine as "the engine of progress."
