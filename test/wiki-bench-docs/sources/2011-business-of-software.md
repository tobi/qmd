# How to make sure your business is on the right side of history through code & culture (Business of Software 2011)

> Raw packet: [[sources/2011-business-of-software]]

## Summary

A 2011 Business of Software talk given when Shopify had ~16,000 stores in 70 countries and had recently raised $22M. Tobi argues there is a "global brainstorm" underway about what a company is for, and that the answer is to **delight customers** rather than maximize cash — money follows critical success, not the reverse. He illustrates with DodoCase (which revived San Francisco bookbinding from 2 to 40 binders in its first year), the NUMMI plant where Toyota's worker autonomy beat GM's model, and Shopify's internal "unicorn" peer-recognition bonus system that distributes 1% of revenue by peer vote.

## Key Points

- "Don't start a company to make money" — money is a side effect of critical success; it can't be sought directly, only followed.
- DodoCase: projected >$4M revenue in year one, and revived an entire trade (SF bookbinding: 2 → 40 full-time binders); a model of entrepreneurship Shopify tries to enable.
- NUMMI lesson: Toyota gave line workers autonomy (a rip cord to stop the line) and rewarded problem-finders; "success doesn't make you right" — yesterday's winning model (GM/Ford) becomes tomorrow's liability.
- Build companies that "won't embarrass us in a hundred years."
- The "bucket of paint test": the org already knows who its most important people are (those others walk to for help); peer recognition surfaces what managers can't see top-down.
- Unicorn: 1% of revenue into a peer-voted bonus pool; surfaced that a "low-performing" hire was actually the company's second-largest bonus recipient because he spent his time helping others.
- Shopify scale at the time: founded 2005, profitable 2008, $22M raised, 16,000 stores / 70 countries, growing 30 → 80 people.

## Counter-arguments

- Talk is a polemic, not data; the bonus-effectiveness experiment is cited loosely.
- Peer-recognition comp (Unicorn) is presented uncritically; no downsides (popularity contests, gaming) discussed here.

## Data gaps

- DodoCase figures ($4M, 40 bookbinders) are as-told, not independently audited.
- The "bonuses reduce long-term output" experiment is referenced without a citation.
